CCA Foundations · Lesson 17 · Domain 5 · Task statement 5.5

Check each part before skipping review

One good average can hide one broken part. Before you let any extractions skip a person, look at each part separately.

Three words used below. An extraction is a value the model reads out of a document, such as an invoice total. Its confidence is a score from 0 to 1 that the model gives each value. A labeled set is a batch of documents where people have already written down the right answers.

The one question to ask before any extraction skips human review: do we know it is accurate for this type of document and this field, not only on average?
No → measure each part first. Yes → let that part skip review, and keep spot-checking it.

An everyday example. A courier says 96.6% of its 1,000 parcels arrive on time. Sounds great. But 900 go to the city, where 891 arrive on time (99%), and 100 go to the hills, where only 75 do (75%). Add them up: 966 out of 1,000 is 96.6%. If you live in the hills, a quarter of your parcels are late, and the headline number never told you. The city is so big that it hides the hills.

1 · How an average hides a broken part

Take a set shaped like the Q4 one: 80% invoices, 15% purchase orders and 5% customs declarations. Suppose invoices are 99% accurate and purchase orders 97%. Then customs declarations could be at 77%, and the overall figure would still come out at exactly 97.6%:

Invoices · 80% of documents99% accurate
POs · 15%97%
Customs77%

0.80 × 99 + 0.15 × 97 + 0.05 × 77 = 79.2 + 14.55 + 3.85 = 97.6. The made-up split shows only that it is possible. In the question you are not told the split, and that is the point: you have to measure it.

The same thing happens inside a document. Every field on an invoice can be fine except one, such as the tax total, and the field average still looks healthy. So the guide asks for accuracy by document type and by field before you reduce human review.

The clue in a stem: when the question tells you the mix (80/15/5, "mostly invoices", "a small share of handwritten forms"), it is asking about the parts, not the total.

2 · The whole process, in order

Task statement 5.5 is one process with four steps. Exam questions usually drop you at one step and offer a fix that belongs to a different one.

1 · Measure each partAccuracy by document type and by field, on a labeled set. An overall figure isn't enough.
2 · Set the barThe model gives a confidence score for each field. Check those scores against the labeled set, and pick the cut-off from what they show.
3 · Route to peopleLow-confidence fields, and documents that are unclear or contradict each other, go to reviewers. Reviewer time is limited, so it goes where errors are likeliest.
4 · Keep checkingTake a random sample of the extractions that skipped review, from every type (stratified random sampling), to track their error rate and catch new kinds of error.

3 · Which confidence score?

Every confidence score comes from the model itself. What makes one usable for routing is two things: it is given per field, and it has been checked against a labeled set (calibrated).

Per-field score, calibrated on a labeled setOne score per document, never checked
What it tells youWhich field is in doubt, with evidence of how often a score like that turns out rightThat something about the document may be off, but not which field, and with no evidence the number means anything
Use it to route review?YesNot yet: calibrate it first, and score each field

You got this right on the Domain 5 paper, Q3 (graded 17 Oct). One related warning: task statement 5.2 calls self-reported confidence an unreliable signal for deciding when to escalate a customer. That's a different decision. For review routing, the guide's answer is to calibrate the score, not to throw it away.

4 · The exception, kept for last: random sampling

Random sampling shows up twice in this topic, once as the wrong answer and once as the right one. What decides it is what the sample is for.

Random sampling used for…VerdictWhy
Choosing what reviewers look at ("review a random 5%")WrongIt spends limited reviewer time evenly on documents that are mostly right. Route by confidence and document problems instead.
Checking the ones nobody reviews (a sample of high-confidence extractions, drawn from every type)RightIt measures the auto-approved error rate and surfaces new kinds of error that nobody else would see.

The exam's name for this is stratified random sampling: a random sample drawn from each type separately. Why bother? A plain 1% sample of 30,000 auto-approved extractions is 300 documents, and only about 15 of those would be customs declarations if they are 5% of the flow. Fifteen is a small number to judge a 77% vs 99% difference from. Sampling each type on its own gives small types their own checks.
