The escalation trigger list

One rule ends every escalation question. You've now missed this three times — in both directions.

Your escalation misses look random until you line them up. On the mock you had an explicit "get me a human" and you tried to solve it yourself — you under-escalated. Just now, a merely-annoyed customer with a routine request, and you escalated — you over-escalated. Same root cause: you're judging each case on feel instead of checking it against a fixed list. This lesson gives you that list, so the feel stops mattering.

Escalate if and only if one of three things is true. Emotion is never one of them.

The three triggers — memorize as a positive list

  1. The customer explicitly asks for a human. Honor it immediately — do not investigate or attempt a fix first. [1]
  2. A policy gap or exception. The policy is silent or ambiguous on this request (e.g. it covers own-site price adjustments but says nothing about matching a competitor). Not the agent's call to invent. [1]
  3. The agent cannot make meaningful progress. It's genuinely stuck, not just facing a hard-but-doable task. [1]

These are NOT triggers — both are unreliable proxies the exam baits you with:

Frustration / sentiment. An upset customer with a simple, in-policy request → acknowledge the frustration and resolve it. Escalate only if they then reiterate they want a human. Sentiment ≠ complexity.
Low self-confidence score. The model is poorly calibrated and confidently wrong on exactly the hard cases — a confidence number is not an escalation signal.

Don't guess — ask for another identifier. (Bonus rule.) [1]
The tell for a policy-gap question: the stem says the policy covers X but is silent on the very thing being asked. Any option where the agent decides the silent case itself — approve, decline, or "ask for proof then decide" — is the wrong-problem distractor. A gap the policy never addressed is escalated, not adjudicated by the agent.

Retrieve it — run the list on each

For every question, silently ask: explicit request? policy gap? stuck? If none → don't escalate. Answer, then read the feedback.

I'm your teacher — ask me anything. Want the edge case where a frustrated customer becomes a valid escalation? Or how "can't make progress" differs from "this is hard"? Ask in the chat — that's where the rule gets sharp.
