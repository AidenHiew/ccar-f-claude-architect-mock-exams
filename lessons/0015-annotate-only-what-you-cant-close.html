CCA Foundations · Lesson 15

Go get it, or say it's missing

One question that would have fixed three of your last four misses.

The one question

Many exam stems describe something missing: a region left out of a report, an empty field, findings people ignore. When you see that, ask:

Can the system still go and get it?
Yes → the right answer goes and gets it.
No → the right answer says honestly that it's missing. No making things up, no hiding it.

The trap is a wrong option that only writes a note saying it's missing when the system could have gone and got it. The note sounds responsible, but the job still isn't done.

An everyday example

Your boss asks for a report covering 6 regions. Two are missing.

If you just didn't look them up, adding "Note: 2 regions missing" at the bottom is a bad answer. Go and look them up.

If the data doesn't exist anywhere, then "Note: no data exists for these 2 regions" is the right answer. Making up numbers, or quietly dropping the regions, would be wrong.

When the note is the right answer

Only when nothing the system does can get the missing thing. These three show up on the exam:

SituationWhy it can't be fetchedRight answer
A contract has no termination clauseIt was never writtenLeave the field empty, so nothing is made up. You got this right on D4 Q1.
The only source refuses access, and nothing else covers itRetrying won't change a refusalDeliver the report and mark that part "gap: source unavailable"
Two trustworthy sources give different numbersThere isn't one true number to findReport both, with where each came from. Don't pick one or average them.

Never right: pretending it's fine

One kind of wrong answer is always wrong: making the problem look like it isn't there. Examples are returning an empty list when the database is actually down (your D2 retake Q7), filling a required field with a guess, or leaving the missing part out so the report "looks complete". An honest note always beats a hidden problem.

One last detail: sometimes "go and get it" means hand it to a person who can. On D4 Q5 the right answer said "send the purchase order, or route it to review". Passing it to someone who can find the missing thing still counts as getting it. Writing "empty" and moving on doesn't.
